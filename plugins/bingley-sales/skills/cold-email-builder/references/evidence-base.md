# Cold Email Skill — Evidence Base

**Purpose:** the citable backbone for the Cold Email skill. When anyone asks "where did you get this?", the answer is here, with a working link and an honest caveat for every claim. This file is the source of truth the rubric weights, the writer, and the grader all trace back to.

**Built:** 15 June 2026. Every entry below was verified against a primary source (peer-reviewed paper, official provider doc, or the original book/vendor page). Figures that are commonly misquoted are flagged in the Corrections Log at the end. Read that section before quoting any number publicly.

---

## How to use the evidence tiers

Not all "best practice" carries equal weight. Every claim is tagged so the rubric can weight honestly:

- **[proven]** — peer-reviewed field experiment or large randomised study. Strongest; causally defensible.
- **[official]** — a rule or measurement published by the platform itself (Google, Yahoo, Microsoft, Apple). Not opinion, it's the operating environment.
- **[large-N vendor]** — big observational dataset from a credible company (Gong, Belkins, Hunter). Directional and correlational, with a commercial incentive. Useful, not gospel.
- **[practitioner]** — consensus among credible operators, little or no controlled data.
- **[heritage]** — foundational books that defined the craft. Authority is reputation and longevity, not a control group.

**Design principle:** score higher-evidence levers more heavily than fad tactics. A rubric that weights an anecdote the same as a randomised field experiment is just opinion with a number stapled on.

# TIER 1 — Books (heritage and modern canon)

The foundational texts the craft is built on. Heritage books are practitioner authority, not controlled evidence, but they are the shared vocabulary and the origin of nearly every modern tactic.

### 1. Eugene M. Schwartz — *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966, Prentice-Hall)
- **Thesis:** You cannot create desire, only channel existing desire. Recognise the prospect's mass desire and the market's state, then write to match it.
- **What the skill uses:** the **Five Stages of Awareness** (Unaware → Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware → Product-Aware → Most-Aware) and the **Five Stages of Market Sophistication**. These decide how direct the opener and subject must be, and why a cold inbox (mostly Problem/Solution-Aware) needs a problem-led message, not a product pitch.
- **Link:** https://openlibrary.org/books/OL8197159M/Breakthrough_Advertising · publisher: https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/breakthrough-advertising-book/
- **Standing:** the most revered strategy text in direct response. [heritage]

### 2. Gary C. Halbert — *The Boron Letters* (written 1984, compiled 2013)
- **Thesis:** Street-level direct-mail craft. Market selection beats everything; copy is salesmanship driven by deep prospect empathy.
- **What the skill uses:** **"The Starving Crowd"** (pick the market/list before the message, the root of "targeting beats copy") and **A-pile / B-pile** mail sorting (people bin obvious solicitations in seconds, so your message must look like personal mail). The print-mail origin of the skill's "reads like a real 1:1 note" realness gate.
- **Link:** original letters free at https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Boron/Boron_Letters_index.htm · editions: https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/17732053-the-boron-letters
- **Standing:** cult classic, near-universally cited. A letter collection, not an academic text. [heritage]

### 3. Claude C. Hopkins — *Scientific Advertising* (1923, public domain)
- **Thesis:** "Advertising is salesmanship." Judge it only by measured results.
- **What the skill uses:** **"salesmanship in print"** (every line must justify itself by selling) and the documented origin of **split-testing and coupon tracking** — the direct ancestor of A/B testing subject lines and sequences.
- **Link:** full text free at https://archive.org/details/scientificadvert0000hopk
- **Standing:** foundational. Ogilvy made his staff read it seven times. [heritage]

### 4. John Caples — *Tested Advertising Methods* (1st ed. 1932, Harper & Brothers; std. 5th ed. 1997, Prentice Hall)
- **Thesis:** Effectiveness can be measured and improved by testing, and the biggest single lever is the headline.
- **What the skill uses:** **headline primacy** — in email the subject line *is* the headline. Caples documented headline swaps producing up to ~19x response. Underpins the rubric treating a broken subject as fatal, not cosmetic.
- **Link:** https://books.google.com/books/about/Tested_Advertising_Methods.html?id=IPdCAAAAIAAJ
- **Standing:** canonical; Caples was a real direct-mail data pioneer. Cite the specific edition you quote (4th 1974 / 5th 1997). [heritage]

### 5. Robert B. Cialdini — *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion* (1984, William Morrow; expanded 2021, Harper Business)
- **Thesis:** Compliance runs on a small set of automatic psychological triggers that can be recognised and ethically deployed.
- **What the skill uses:** the **six principles** (Reciprocity, Commitment/Consistency, Social Proof, Authority, Liking, Scarcity); the 2021 edition adds **Unity**. Each maps to a cold-email move. Note: "it's in *Influence*" is a taxonomy, not itself proof; each principle's strength is graded separately in Tier 2.
- **Link:** https://www.influenceatwork.com/books-and-publications/
- **Standing:** the most-cited popular book on persuasion; grounded in peer-reviewed work, the strongest academic pedigree among the books. [heritage] / [proven] for the underlying studies

### 6. Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler — *Predictable Revenue* (2011, PebbleStorm)
- **Thesis:** Outbound becomes predictable when you specialise sales roles and systematise cold outreach.
- **What the skill uses:** **"Cold Calling 2.0"** — short, plain-text, referral-style emails sent high in the org that ask to be pointed to the right person rather than pitching. The template for modern B2B cold email: short, no HTML, low-friction ask.
- **Link:** https://predictablerevenue.com/blog/15-minute-summary-of-predictable-revenue/
- **Standing:** defined the modern SaaS/SDR outbound model. **Note:** the book's term is "Cold Calling 2.0," not "Cold Email 2.0" — quote it correctly. [practitioner]

### 7. Robert Collier — *The Robert Collier Letter Book* (1931, public domain in many jurisdictions)
- **Thesis:** Sales letters work by meeting readers inside their own existing concerns.
- **What the skill uses:** **"Enter the conversation already taking place in the customer's mind"** — open where the prospect's attention already is, not with your product. The foundation of a relevant, personalised opener (the heaviest scored dimension).
- **Link:** https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/2670284-the-robert-collier-letter-book
- **Standing:** direct-mail classic. The famous line is a faithful paraphrase of his teaching, not one verbatim sentence; attribute as "the idea Collier is known for." Original publisher not cleanly verifiable. [heritage]

### 8. Robert W. Bly — *The Copywriter's Handbook* (1st ed. 1985; 4th ed. 2020, Holt/Macmillan)
- **Thesis:** Effective copy is a learnable, systematic craft: clear, benefit-driven, built to drive action.
- **What the skill uses:** **benefits over features** (translate every feature into a concrete reader outcome) and the **Motivating Sequence** (attention → need → satisfy → prove → action), a usable skeleton for a cold-email body.
- **Link:** https://us.macmillan.com/books/9781250238016/thecopywritershandbook/ · 1st ed: https://archive.org/details/copywritershandb00blyr
- **Standing:** the standard practical primer for working copywriters, in print ~40 years. [heritage]

### 9. Jeb Blount — *Fanatical Prospecting* (2015, Wiley)
- **Thesis:** Pipeline is everything; the only cure for an empty pipeline is relentless multi-channel prospecting. Consistent activity beats clever tricks.
- **What the skill uses:** the **email-prospecting test** — an email must get opened, get read, and get a response — and the framing of email as one channel inside a balanced cadence, not a standalone silver bullet.
- **Link:** https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Fanatical+Prospecting-p-9781119144755
- **Standing:** best-selling modern prospecting book (500k+ copies). The default citable modern reference for prospecting discipline. [practitioner]

### 10. Mike Weinberg — *New Sales. Simplified.* (2012, AMACOM)
- **Thesis:** New-business development isn't mysterious: pick target accounts, build a customer-focused sales story, prospect proactively.
- **What the skill uses:** the **"power statement"/sales story** — lead with the prospect's problems and the outcomes you create, not your company. The messaging backbone for a strong value prop.
- **Link:** https://mikeweinberg.com/new-sales-simplified/
- **Standing:** HubSpot "Top 20 Sales Book of All Time"; staple of the outbound canon. [practitioner]

# TIER 2 — Peer-reviewed studies (the proven core)

Every figure below was checked against the original paper. These are the claims the skill can defend as science, not folklore.

### A. Sahni, Wheeler & Chintagunta (2018) — email personalisation [proven]
- **Citation:** *Personalization in Email Marketing: The Role of Noninformative Advertising Content.* Marketing Science, 37(2), 236–258.
- **Design:** large-scale **randomised field experiments**, three real partners, millions of emails. Top evidence tier.
- **Finding:** the recipient's **name in the subject line** raised open probability **+20%** (9.05% → 10.80%), lifted **leads +31%** (0.39% → 0.51%), and **cut unsubscribes −17%**.
- **Caveat:** opt-in/house lists, not pure cold. The mechanism (signalling attention) transfers; the exact magnitudes may not. Base rates are small.
- **Link:** https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2017.1066
- **Skill use:** the single proven justification for the Subject Line dimension and for personalisation generally.

### B. Langer, Blank & Chanowitz (1978) — the "because" / copy-machine study [proven]
- **Citation:** *The Mindlessness of Ostensibly Thoughtful Action.* JPSP, 36(6), 635–642. Field experiment, n=120.
- **Finding (exact, from the original):**
  - Small favour (5 pages): request only **60%**, placebic "because I have to make copies" **93%**, real reason **94%**.
  - Large favour (20 pages): request only **24%**, placebic **24%**, real reason **42%**.
- **Significance:** main effect F(2,108)=3.02, p<.05; the large-favour contrast was only a trend (.05<p<.10).
- **The real lesson:** give a **real reason for a real ask.** The "any 'because' works" version is folklore — at the high-effort level the empty reason collapsed back to the no-reason rate.
- **Link:** https://jamesclear.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/copy-machine-study-ellen-langer.pdf
- **Skill use:** rewards a genuine "why now / why you" in the CTA; refuses to reward a content-free reason.

### C. Huang, Yeomans, Brooks, Minson & Gino (2017) — question-asking increases liking [proven]
- **Citation:** *It Doesn't Hurt to Ask: Question-Asking Increases Liking.* JPSP, 113(3), 430–452.
- **Design:** >600 online conversations plus a **speed-dating field study** (110 people, 2,000+ conversations, NLP-coded).
- **Finding:** asking more questions, especially **follow-up** questions, increases liking via perceived responsiveness; in the field it predicted more second dates (a behavioural outcome).
- **Caveat:** effect is specific to follow-up questions in a two-way conversation. Applying it to one-way cold email is a reasonable inference, not something the study tested.
- **Link:** https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Huang%20et%20al%202017_6945bc5e-3b3e-4c0a-addd-254c9e603c60.pdf
- **Skill use:** supports a genuine question as a low-friction CTA and the consultative "poke the bear" move.

### D. Defau, Zauner & Sycik (2023) — authority (CEO sender) in email [proven, weak]
- **Citation:** *How to Improve the Performance of Email Marketing Campaigns? A Field Experiment Using the Authority Principle.* Review of Marketing Science, 21(1), 187–194.
- **Design:** two randomised field experiments; only the sender line differed ("Joe Doe" vs "Joe Doe, CEO").
- **Finding:** Experiment 1 (prospects) opens **+9.8%**, CTR **+75%**, all significant; **but** unsubscribes **+177%**. Experiment 2 (existing customers) same direction, **not significant**.
- **Caveat:** the weakest study here. Single anonymous company; effect held only with prospects; authority **polarises** (raises unsubscribes). Treat the credible-sender lift as suggestive.
- **Link:** https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/roms-2022-0095/html
- **Skill use:** supports a credible sender identity in Proof & Credibility, with a warning not to overplay authority.

### E. Goldstein, Cialdini & Griskevicius (2008) — social norms / hotel towels [proven, contested]
- **Citation:** *A Room with a Viewpoint.* Journal of Consumer Research, 35(3), 472–482. Two field experiments.
- **Finding:** a **descriptive-norm** message ("~75% of guests reuse their towels") produced 44.1% reuse vs 35.1% for a standard appeal (~9 points, ~26% relative); **provincial** norms (same room) beat general norms.
- **Caveat:** modest effect, and a notable **partial replication failure** (Bohner & Schlüter 2014, PLOS ONE). Cite as "influential but contested."
- **Link:** https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/35/3/472/1856257 · replication: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0104086
- **Skill use:** rewards **specific, similar** social proof (a named peer in the same segment) over generic logos.

### Foundational compliance experiments

**F. Freedman & Fraser (1966) — foot-in-the-door [proven].** JPSP 4(2), 195–202. Exp 1 (phone): small request first → large-request compliance **52.8% vs 22.2%** control. Exp 2 (door-to-door): similar small ask → **76.0% vs 16.7%** control. Don't blend the two experiments into a single "17%→76%." https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Influence_Compliance/Freedman_Fraser_Foot-in-the-door.pdf
*Skill use:* the tiny-yes-before-the-real-ask logic behind low-friction CTAs.

**G. Cialdini et al. (1975) — door-in-the-face [proven].** JPSP 31(2), 206–215. Zoo-trip request after a large refusal: **50%** compliance vs **16.7%** control. Requires same source, close in time, visible concession. https://doi.org/10.1037/h0076284
*Skill use:* understood as a mechanic, but see the meta-analysis caveat below before relying on it.

**H. Regan (1971) — reciprocity [proven].** J. Experimental Social Psychology 7(6), 627–639. An unsolicited favour (a free Coke) raised compliance regardless of liking; the driver was a felt obligation to reciprocate. Dated, all-male lab sample. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0022103171900254
*Skill use:* the evidence under "give value first" / the offer-based CTA.

**I. Strohmetz, Rind, Fisher & Lynn (2002) — reciprocity applied (restaurant mints) [proven].** J. Applied Social Psychology 32(2), 300–309. Exp 1 (n=92): candy with the check lifted tips **15.1% → 17.8%** (p<.0001). Exp 2: an extra "1+1" gesture beat simply giving two pieces, reaching **~23%** — supporting reciprocity over mood. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1559-1816.2002.tb00216.x
*Skill use:* a small, clean real-world demonstration that an unexpected gift lifts compliance — the offer-CTA rationale.

### Meta-analyses (effect-size reality check)
- **Foot-in-the-door:** Beaman et al. (1983), ~120 conditions — real but **weak**, roughly half showed no/reverse effect. Burger (1999) — works but heavily moderated. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167283092002
- **Door-in-the-face:** Feeley, Anker & Aloe (2012) — significant on **verbal** compliance (r=.126) but **non-significant on behavioural** compliance (r=.052). Key caveat: DITF moves what people say, much less what they do. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03637751.2012.697631
- **Head-to-head:** Pascual & Guéguen (2005) — both beat control, no significant difference between them. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/pr0.96.1.122-128

**Why the meta-analyses matter:** they stop the skill over-selling persuasion tricks. These techniques are real but small and moderated. The skill weights them as supporting colour, never as primary levers.

# TIER 3 — Large-N vendor datasets (directional)

Big real-world samples from credible companies. Treat as directional: commercial incentive, undisclosed methodology, key figures often in chart images. Strong for direction, weak for precise magnitude.

### J. Gong — cold-email CTA study (~304,174 emails, 2020) [large-N vendor]
- **Finding:** an **interest** CTA ("Open to learning more?") wins on a **cold** first touch; a **specific day/time** CTA wins **in-deal**. Gong's own line: meetings booked rise "from **15% in the cold email stage to 37% in the deal stage**" when matched to stage.
- **Caveat:** the three-way percentage splits live **only in a chart image**; quote the 15%→37% (which is in body text) and the directional rule. Six years old. Some widely circulated splits ("12% vs 7% replies") are **third-party inventions, not Gong's** — do not attribute them to Gong.
- **Link:** https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings
- **Skill use:** the spine of the CTA dimension and the "no cold calendar link" rule.

### K. Gong — "does cold email still work" (~28M emails, 2025) [large-N vendor]
- **Finding:** **pitching reduces replies by as much as 57%.** Ideal length under 100 words / 3–4 sentences. Buzzwords and numbers cut opens up to 17.9%. Average rep sends 344 emails per meeting.
- **Caveat:** vendor platform data promoting a paid course; no confidence intervals or per-cohort n. The page cites both "28M+" and "25M" — quote as "~28M." The "problem-first +20% / social proof +41%" lifts are **not** in this study (they're in the 85M writeup below).
- **Link:** https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says
- **Skill use:** evidence for problem-led over pitch, and for brevity.

### L. 30MPC / Outbound Squad — data-backed formula (~85M emails, 2025) [large-N vendor]
- **Finding:** a 4-sentence "Reply Method": personalised opener → problem/priority → social proof → offer CTA. Lifts (chart-image): **problem-first +20%, social proof +41%, personalisation up to 5x, offer-based CTA +28%, pitching −57%.**
- **Caveat:** same underlying vendor dataset as the two Gong studies (not independent corroboration); figures are chart-image plus bold text; drives a paid course.
- **Link:** https://www.30mpc.com/newsletter/the-data-backed-cold-email-formula-the-exact-words-length
- **Skill use:** the structural template for Write mode and the offer-CTA tier.

### M. Belkins — B2B reply rates (16.5M emails, 93 domains, 2024) [large-N vendor]
- **Finding:** average reply rate **5.8% in 2024, down from 6.8% in 2023** (a 15% YoY drop). Best-documented broad benchmark.
- **Caveat:** agency's own client book (sells appointment-setting), not market-wide. Sub-segment peaks (single-email ~8.4%) shouldn't be conflated with the 5.8% average.
- **Link:** https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates
- **Skill use:** the realistic reply-rate anchor (~5–6% average) the skill uses to calibrate expectations.

### N. Hunter.io — relevance / personalisation (~11M emails + survey, 2025) [large-N vendor]
- **Finding:** relevance is the #1 reply driver; **71% of surveyed decision-makers** say they don't reply because emails don't address a relevant problem; small segments (≤100) beat blasts.
- **Caveat:** vendor data plus survey. The widely-quoted "52% higher replies / 2.76x" figures are **SEO-blog embellishments, not Hunter's published numbers** — use the 11M sample, the 71% stat, and the qualitative direction only.
- **Link:** https://hunter.io/blog/making-your-cold-emails-relevant-101 · study: https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email/
- **Skill use:** supports Relevance & Targeting as the heaviest dimension.

### S. Boomerang — email length, reading level & questions (~40M emails, 2016) [large-N vendor]
- **Finding:** across ~40 million emails, messages of **50–125 words** had the best response rates (just over **50%**); writing at a **3rd-grade reading level** earned **~36% more responses** than college level (and ~17% more than high-school level); including **1–3 questions** lifted reply likelihood by **~50%** over asking none.
- **Caveat:** vendor data (Boomerang sells an email tool), observational not randomised, mixed business/personal mail rather than pure cold B2B. The figures are widely quoted accurately; the lever is directional. Use 50–125 words and the 3rd-grade level as **ceilings, not targets**. Not peer-reviewed — do **not** tag [proven].
- **Link:** https://blog.boomerangapp.com/2016/02/7-tips-for-getting-more-responses-to-your-emails-with-data/
- **Skill use:** the meter's word-count floor (Brevity §9 T1), the reading-grade ceiling (Brevity §9 T3), and the 1–3 question flag (§9 T2).

### T. Yesware — subject-line length & sales templates (262,518 templates; 115M emails) [large-N vendor]
- **Finding:** analysing **262,518** sales-email templates, open/reply rates peaked with subject lines of **1–5 words** (ideal ~3) and fell away **beyond ~7 words**; a separate **115M-email** analysis found subjects containing **numbers** opened ~45% above average. Yesware's own note: subject *length* is a weak lever versus relevance.
- **Caveat:** vendor data (Yesware sells outreach software), observational, sales-template population. Two different samples — don't merge the 262,518 templates with the 115M-email numbers study. Not peer-reviewed; use as a soft flag, never a dock.
- **Link:** https://www.yesware.com/blog/email-subject-line-analysis/ · templates: https://www.yesware.com/blog/analyzed-data-sales-templates/
- **Skill use:** the meter's subject-word-count flags (1–6 fine · 7–9 slightly long · ≥10 trim; §9 T2) — never docks.

# TIER 4 — Official deliverability rules (the operating environment)

These are not opinions; they are the rules the inbox enforces. The skill assumes they're handled and gates on the copy-level signals that interact with them. All current as of June 2026.

### O. Google — bulk sender guidelines [official]
- **Rule:** senders of **~5,000+/day to personal Gmail** need SPF + DKIM + DMARC, From-alignment, one-click unsubscribe. Keep spam rate **below 0.10%**, never reach **0.30%**.
- **2025 update:** from **November 2025** Gmail ramped enforcement — non-compliant mail now faces temporary and permanent rejections, plus a new Compliance Status Dashboard. The 5k threshold applies to @gmail.com, not Google Workspace inboxes.
- **Link:** https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414
- **Skill use:** the deliverability gate assumes auth is done; flags if the user hasn't.

### P. Yahoo — sender requirements [official]
- **Rule:** matches Google's Feb-2024 baseline: SPF + DKIM + DMARC, alignment, one-click unsubscribe, spam **below 0.3%**, DKIM ≥1024-bit. Honour unsubscribes within 2 days.
- **Link:** https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/
- **Skill use:** same gate; confirms the baseline is industry-wide, not one provider.

### Q. Microsoft — Outlook high-volume sender rules [official]
- **Rule:** effective **5 May 2025**, senders of **5,000+/day** to outlook.com/hotmail.com/live.com need SPF + DKIM + DMARC, alignment, one-click unsubscribe.
- **Enforcement reality:** as of June 2026 non-compliant mail is **routed to Junk**, not rejected. Full rejection is a planned later phase. (Third-party blogs claiming rejection began 5 May are wrong — Microsoft walked that back before launch.)
- **Link:** https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlooks-new-requirements-for-highvolume-senders/4399730
- **Skill use:** confirms the big three consumer providers now share one baseline.

### R. Apple Mail Privacy Protection — why open rate is dead [official measurement / vendor analysis]
- **Finding:** MPP (since 2021) pre-fetches images, firing the open pixel whether or not a human reads the email. Validity (2024): Apple's proxy alone was **~73% of pixel-fire events**.
- **Caveat:** 73% is share of *pixel-fire events*, North-America-weighted and time-bound to 2024, not "73% of all email." Use the precise framing.
- **Link:** https://www.validity.com/blog/case-closed-the-mystery-of-declining-email-open-rates/
- **Skill use:** the reason the skill scores subject lines but never uses open rate as a KPI; **reply rate is the north star.**

# TIER 5 — Inbox placement and legal compliance (the new gates)

Added 15 June 2026. Two things every competitor ignores: where the email actually lands, and whether it's legal. Both are gates, not quality points.

### Placement — how the providers sort cold mail
- **No "cold" or "prospecting" category exists.** Google and Microsoft fold all unsolicited mail into one class; there is no prospecting exception. The lever they actually enforce on is **complaint rate (below 0.1%, 1 per 1,000)**, not volume. Low-volume, plain-text, personal-looking mail *can* place better, but only if engagement stays positive and complaints stay near zero. [official]
- **Gmail tabs:** Promotions is triggered by bulk/marketing signals (heavy HTML, images, multiple links, tracking pixels, a List-Unsubscribe header, brand-only sender name). Plain text, a personal sender name and minimal links trend to Primary. Behaviour-inferred; Google doesn't publish the classifier.
- **Outlook Focused vs Other:** ML sorts by who you interact with and pushes bulk/noisy mail to Other; user training overrides it. https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/what-is-focused-inbox-16b24373-dfa9-4139-ab19-08aa753a6055
- **Microsoft Defender:** SCL (Spam Confidence Level) 5–6 routes to Junk, 7–9 to high-confidence spam (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-spam-spam-confidence-level-scl-about). BCL (Bulk Complaint Level) runs 0–9; default Junk threshold is BCL 7, Standard preset 6, Strict 5. "Bulk = graymail." https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/anti-spam-bulk-complaint-level-bcl-about
- **Note:** the "warm up new inboxes at 10–20/day" rule is vendor/community lore, not an official Google or Microsoft requirement. Don't cite it as policy.

### Legal — what binds a cold B2B sender
- **CAN-SPAM (US):** applies to all commercial email, **no B2B exemption.** Requires accurate headers/subject, a valid physical postal address, and a working opt-out honoured within 10 days. Consent is **not** required. Penalties up to ~$53,088 per email. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- **UK PECR:** the electronic-mail consent rule **does not apply to corporate subscribers** (Ltd companies, LLPs), so true B2B cold email to those is allowed; **sole traders and partnerships count as individuals** and need consent or soft opt-in. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/
- **UK/EU GDPR:** still applies to the personal data (name, email) regardless of PECR; needs a lawful basis (usually legitimate interests) plus the right to object. EU ePrivacy is stricter and varies by member state, **verify per country.** [official, jurisdiction-dependent]

**Skill use:** a **compliance gate** (physical address present, working opt-out, defensible lawful basis) and **placement signals** folded into the deliverability/realness gate. The legal layer is the clearest value-add no competitor offers.

**Funnel logic (why some dimensions are gates, not line items):** cold email is a sequential funnel — subject gates the open (Caples, Sahni), opener gates the read (Collier, Halbert), CTA gates the reply (Gong, Langer). A broken link in the chain kills the email regardless of the rest, which is why Subject, CTA and Deliverability act as gates that cap the total, not as additive points. The weighted score only ranks quality among emails that clear the gates.

**Calibration status:** the weights above are evidence-informed **starting values**, not yet validated against a graded email set. The calibration step (blind-grade 20–40 real emails, tune weights/anchors/cut-offs until the skill's ranking matches human judgement) is what turns them from defensible estimates into tuned numbers.

# Corrections Log — numbers to never misquote

The research pass caught these errors in circulation (some in our own earlier synthesis). Fixing them is what keeps the skill credible when challenged.

1. **Predictable Revenue** — the term is **"Cold Calling 2.0,"** not "Cold Email 2.0." The mechanic is referral-style cold email, but quote the real phrase.
2. **Langer "because" study** — does **not** prove "any reason works." The placebic reason collapsed to the no-reason rate (24% vs 24%) on high-effort asks. Real lesson: a real reason for a real ask.
3. **Strohmetz mints study** — the real figures are **15.1% → 17.8%** (Exp 1) and up to **~23%** (Exp 2). It is **not** "3% / 14% / 21%"; the floating "21%" is a revenue figure, not a tip rate.
4. **Freedman & Fraser** — don't merge the two experiments. "53% vs 22%" (Exp 1) and "76% vs 16.7%" (Exp 2) are different studies.
5. **Gong CTA splits** — "interest 12% vs 7% replies / 68% vs 41% positive" are **third-party inventions**, not in Gong's article. Use only Gong's own "15% → 37% meetings" and the directional rule.
6. **Gong 28M study** — the "+20% problem-first / +41% social proof" lifts belong to the **85M 30MPC** writeup, not the 28M study. Don't cross-attribute.
7. **Hunter.io** — "52% higher replies / 2.76x" are **SEO-blog embellishments**, not Hunter's numbers. Use the 11M sample and the 71% survey stat.
8. **Microsoft enforcement** — as of June 2026 non-compliant high-volume mail is **junk-foldered, not rejected.** Rejection is a later phase.
9. **Goldstein 2008 towels** — flag as **contested** (failed a 2014 replication), not settled.
10. **Apple/Validity 73%** — that is share of **pixel-fire events** (2024, NA-weighted), not 73% of all email or all users.
11. **Boomerang tier** — the 40M-email findings (50–125 words, 3rd-grade +36%, 1–3 questions ~50%) are **[large-N vendor]**, not [proven]. The figures are real and correctly quoted; only the earlier "[proven]" tag in scoring-model §9 was wrong (now corrected). Vendor, observational, directional.
12. **Yesware samples** — the subject-line study analysed **262,518 templates** (the "262k"); the **numbers-in-subject ~45% opens** stat is from a **separate 115M-email** analysis. Don't merge the two.
